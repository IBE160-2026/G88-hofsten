# PRD Reconciliation Review

## Verdict

**Partial reconciliation.** The spine follows the approved PRD's architecture-level decisions and has no direct contradiction with its approved requirements. It omits or weakens several exact calculation, presentation, verification, and scope-caveat contracts that the PRD makes explicit.

## Authority

The PRD frontmatter says `status: approved`. Its `.memlog.md` records user approval after the second Validate result (Excellent, zero critical/high/medium; the one low finding was accepted after correction). These are authoritative. PRD §0's sentence calling the document a draft and “not approved or final” is stale; it does not reverse the approval or invalidate the PRD. The spine's note in Deferred correctly recognizes that stale sentence.

## Findings

1. **The numerical cost-model contract is underspecified.** PRD §4.2 FR-5 fixes the speed-to-fuel equation, partial-day proration, berth-wait burn, USD 600/tonne price, excluded delay and port-stay fuel, 8–13-knot inclusive per-leg feasibility, excluded commercial costs, exact 100/11 calculation speed, and rounding/time rules. Spine AD-4 says to implement the “approved cost-model formulas and berth-window rules” and refers generally to a precision boundary, but does not carry these decisive rules into the spine. A reader implementing from the spine could choose materially different calculations while believing they had followed it.

2. **The three scenario definitions are not fully carried into the spine.** PRD §1 and §4.2 FR-4 specify A–B speeds of 10, 12.5, and exactly 100/11 knots, require B–C to remain at 10 knots, and constrain the reduced-speed label and berth-window claim. The spine calls them “three fixed responses” (Capability → Architecture Map) and names 10 knots in AD-5, but does not state the complete speed/leg mapping or the reduced-speed labeling constraint. Preserve the exact sample-only scenario contract so implementations do not generalize or alter it.

3. **Delay validation lacks the PRD's complete invalid-input contract.** PRD §4.1 FR-2 explicitly rejects empty, non-numeric/non-finite, fractional, negative, and over-720 values without clamping or silently adjusting them. Spine AD-3 and Consistency Conventions establish the inclusive integer range and generic rejection without clamping, but do not enumerate these invalid classes or make them explicit validation-test cases. This leaves input coercion and boundary behavior open to inconsistent implementations.

4. **The berth-window boundary oracle is reduced to a general rule.** PRD §4.2 FR-3 specifies inclusive opening/exclusive closing, expected B arrival/wait values at 0, 6, and 12 hours, the 5:59/6:00/6:01 transition, and that no arrival reaches the second window's close within the current input range. The spine gives the open/closed rule and asks for 0-, 6-, and 12-hour oracles, but does not retain the minute-boundary checks or envelope caveat. Keep these cases explicit in the architecture verification contract; a generic rule alone can miss an off-by-one implementation.

5. **The explanation's required content and qualitative limits are missing.** PRD §4.3 FR-6 requires an explanation to cite displayed ETA, berth-wait, and cost results; discuss both time and cost trade-offs, including a more-expensive earlier option; state simulation and unmodelled-contract/emissions limitations; and treat qualitative factors as matters to check, not assessed risks. Spine AD-6 constrains the provider to explanatory text over calculated results, but does not specify these content obligations or explicitly exclude risk scoring. The architecture therefore does not protect the PRD's explanation quality and scope boundary.

6. **Fallback behavior is specified by route, not by its full user-visible contract.** PRD §4.3 FR-7 requires the exact canonical 12-hour copy and label, matching numeric claims with no added or contradictory numbers, a clearly named rule-based fallback at other delays, visible scenario results, explicit notice that no live explanation was produced, and no fallback retained from a previous delay. Spine AD-6 and the capability map preserve mock-versus-rule-based selection generally, and verification mentions canonical copy, but omit these exact display and stale-state requirements. “Current-results-only” does not by itself guarantee the required labels, visibility, or numeric fidelity.

7. **Simulated-data and decision-boundary labels are not an architectural presentation requirement.** PRD §4.1 FR-1, §4.4, and §7 SM-3 require visible text distinguishing simulated figures from live data and real commercial costs, identifying live AI versus mock versus rule-based explanations, and making clear that the planner decides; those distinctions cannot rely on colour alone. The spine says the application is a demo and that the planner retains control (AD-6 and the FR-8 map), but does not require these visible, color-independent labels. A compliant internal policy could still ship a UI that fails SM-3.

8. **SM-1's recommendation/fallback verification matrix is not explicit.** PRD §7 SM-1 requires checking both live recommendations and rule-based recommendations against eligibility, unrounded cost, and tie-break rules at representative 0-, 6-, and 12-hour delays, plus the 12-hour mock and non-baseline fallback. Spine Verification calls for policy tests and the three delay oracles, and mentions provider success/missing-key/failure paths, but does not require this per-delay, per-path recommendation matrix or verify fallback labels, scenario visibility, and numeric claims. State those assertions so broad test categories cannot be mistaken for coverage.

9. **Repeated-edit reproducibility is a requirement but not a stated test sequence.** PRD §4.1 FR-2 and §7 SM-2 require the sequence 12 hours → 6 hours → 0 hours → 12 hours to reproduce the same complete 12-hour results, with no cumulative shift. Spine AD-3 correctly requires recomputation from immutable source data, but its Verification convention does not ask for this sequence. Add it as an explicit request/rendering or integration test; testing isolated delays alone will not detect history-dependent state.

10. **The anti-false-precision counter-metric is absent.** PRD §7 SM-C1 says not to optimize persuasive force or apparent certainty at the expense of simulated-data labeling, numeric fidelity, or planner authority. The spine covers pieces of this in AD-6, but neither names the counter-metric nor preserves its balancing purpose alongside recommendation and clarity goals. This omission weakens an explicit quality guardrail when presentation or AI behavior is later tuned.

11. **The project-process criterion is not recorded as out of product scope.** PRD §7 SP-1 requires project records to document AI-assisted and agentic development and explicitly says this is a process criterion, not a user-facing feature. The spine binds SM-1 through SM-3 but says nothing about SP-1. It need not become an application capability, but should be identified as a separate project-delivery obligation or explicitly outside the architecture's implementation scope.

12. **The user-validation caveat is missing.** PRD §2.1 and §9 state that Emma and the target-user needs are hypothetical/inferred and unvalidated; §6.2 says V1 makes no claim of proven user value and excludes external validation. The spine describes a demonstration and non-production use but does not carry this evidence limitation. Without it, later readers may mistake the architecture's planner-facing workflow for validated user requirements.
